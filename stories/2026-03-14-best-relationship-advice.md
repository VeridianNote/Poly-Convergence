Here it is:

> Relationships only work when the people in them want to be there.<br/>
It only takes one of them to end it.

Sit with it for a second. It's the best relationship advice you'll ever get.<br/>
It seems simple and obvious on the surface, but there's so much more to unpack past the first glance.

<!-- truncate -->

At first glance, you might read it as "you can't force anyone to stay in a relationship."<br/>
That's true — but the wisdom in that little nugget goes so much further.<br/>
To me it dictates the reason I should always *want* to keep trying to make my partners happy.

It's actually simple logic at its core:

- I can't make anyone choose anything.
- I want my partners to choose to be here.
- I can help make their choices worthwhile.

It highlights that relationships take effort.<br/>
But what does that really mean?

It's not about the effort of putting up with people.<br/>
It's about the effort of making yourself the best version of you — so that others choose to be there.<br/>
That's the key.

And once you see it that way, it becomes a mirror.

If you want kindness, be kind.<br/>
If you want respect, give it.<br/>
If you want someone who loves deeply, be willing to love deeply yourself.<br/>
If you want vulnerability, go first.

Be someone worth choosing. Not by changing who you are — by being more of who you already are at your best.
