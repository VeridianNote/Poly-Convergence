# Contribute

This site gets better every time someone shares what they know. If you've learned something from navigating non-monogamous relationships -- about communication, about what works, about what doesn't -- there's a good chance someone else needs to hear it.

You don't need to be an expert. You don't need to be a writer. You just need experience and honesty.

## What you can contribute

- **Wiki articles** -- Reference content covering relationship concepts, communication tools, research summaries, and practical guidance. These are the building blocks other people will come back to.
- **Blog posts** -- Longer pieces exploring a topic, examining a claim, or sharing a perspective. More personal voice, more room to dig in.
- **Community stories** -- First-hand accounts from your own experience. These don't need to have a tidy ending -- they need to be real.
- **Corrections and improvements** -- Spot something wrong or incomplete? Fix it. That's just as valuable as writing something new.

## How it works

The easiest way to contribute is through our [web editor](/contribute). Sign in with GitHub and start writing -- no Git knowledge required.

Every submission goes through editorial review before it goes live. This isn't gatekeeping -- it's quality control. We check for accuracy, tone, and privacy (no real names or identifying details about anyone). The goal is making sure the site stays useful and trustworthy, not filtering out voices.

If you prefer working with Git directly, you can open a pull request on the [repository](https://github.com/VeridianNote/Poly-Convergence).

## A few ground rules

These aren't complicated. They're common sense for a community resource:

- **Be honest.** Share what you actually know and experienced. If you're speculating, say so.
- **Source your claims.** If you're citing research or making factual statements, link to your sources where possible. It's fine to say "I don't have a source for this, but here's what I've observed" -- just be clear about the difference.
- **Protect privacy.** Don't include real names, identifying details, or anything that could out someone -- yours or anyone else's. Stories can be powerful without being traceable.
- **Keep images under 2MB.** EXIF data (location, device info) is stripped automatically for your privacy.
