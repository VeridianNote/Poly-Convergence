# Terms of Contribution

When you submit content to Poly Convergence — whether through a pull request or the web editor — here's how the licensing works. This isn't a contract full of scary clauses. It's just a plain explanation of what happens to your work when you share it here.

## You Keep What You Create

Contributing to this site doesn't transfer ownership of anything. You keep full copyright over everything you write. These terms are about how we can use your work to run the site — not about taking it from you.

## What You Grant Us

By submitting content, you give Poly Convergence a perpetual, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to host, display, and distribute your contribution on this site and through related channels (RSS feeds, social previews, etc.). This is the minimum we need to publish your work. It doesn't prevent you from publishing the same piece elsewhere or doing anything else you want with it.

## Blog Post License Options

For community stories and blog posts, you choose how your work can be used beyond this site. Two options are available:

**CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (Default)**
Your writing can be shared and adapted by *others*, as long as they give you credit, don't use it commercially, and share any adaptations under the same license. The "NonCommercial" clause restricts what others can do — not what you can do. You're the copyright holder, so you can still publish the same piece elsewhere, monetize it, or license it differently. This is a good fit for educational content you want to help spread — guides, essays, personal reflections on navigating polyamory.

**All Rights Reserved**
Think of it like renting out a room in your house — you set the rules for guests, but those rules don't apply to you. You still own the house. With All Rights Reserved, *others* can link to your piece and quote short excerpts under fair use, but they can't repost or redistribute the full text without your permission. Meanwhile, you can do whatever you want with your own work — publish it on Substack, rework it into a book chapter, sell it, license it to a magazine. The restrictions only apply to everyone else, never to you. This is a good fit if you're a professional writer, plan to publish the piece elsewhere, or just want tighter control over how your work circulates.

> If you don't specify a license when submitting, your post defaults to CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

## Wiki Content

All wiki contributions are published under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — this isn't optional. Wiki pages are community resources, not personal essays. They're meant to be shared, translated, quoted, and built upon. Keeping them under a permissive license is what makes that possible.

If you want to write something personal with tighter control over redistribution, submit it as a blog post instead.

## Images and Uploads

If you upload an image or other media with your submission, you're representing that you have the right to use it. Specifically, that means one of the following:

- **You created it** — and you retain full rights to it
- **It's from the public domain or under a permissive license** — and you've reviewed the terms and confirmed they allow this use
- **You have explicit permission** from the rights holder — and can demonstrate that if asked

Screenshots of other people's posts, social media content, and similar material may qualify as fair use depending on context — but fair use is a legal gray area. When in doubt, don't upload it. Stock photo watermarked previews are never acceptable.

## Your Responsibility

By submitting content, you're representing that:

- You have the right to submit it
- It doesn't infringe on anyone else's copyright, trademark, or other rights (beyond what's covered by fair use)
- It doesn't contain anything you're under a legal obligation to keep private

If something you submit causes a rights problem, that's on you — not on Poly Convergence or its maintainers. We will remove any content that is found to violate the rights of others.

## Site Artwork and Branding

The site's logos, illustrations, and visual branding are all rights reserved and are not available for reuse, adaptation, or commercial distribution without permission. To request permission, [open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/VeridianNote/Poly-Convergence/issues).

## Moderation

We reserve the right to edit, reject, or remove contributions that violate community guidelines or don't fit the site's purpose. For substantive edits, we'll try to work with you before making changes. For things that clearly cross the line — hate speech, harassment, deliberate misinformation — we'll remove without negotiation.

Moderation decisions are made by the site maintainers. If you have a concern about how your content was handled, you can reach out through the site.
